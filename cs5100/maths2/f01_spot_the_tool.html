Final: spot the shared maths

A mixed test across all eight weeks. Most exam questions are one of a dozen calculations in disguise. This stage first practises recognising which one, with a worked "how I spotted it" for each, then collects the common slips from every stage on one page, then tests you on fresh numbers.

How to use this stage

Read section 1 and do the warm-ups. Then skim the slip list in section 2: each line is a mistake that cost marks somewhere in this route. The final test draws 14 questions from a larger pool and needs 70%. Retake it as often as you like; the fresh-number questions change every time, and each one shows the full worked table when you check it. Before starting, try writing the twelve formulas of the toolkit table from memory on one sheet. That sheet is also most of a good exam crib.

1. Spotting the tool

In plain words

Read the question for the noun, not the model name. “Distance” or “nearest” → A1. “Average”, “mean”, “centre”, “vote” → A2. “Squared”, “RSS”, “SSE”, “stress” → A3. “Update”, “learning rate”, “gradient” → A4. “Penalty”, “complexity”, “\(\lambda\)”, “\(\alpha\)” → A5. “Given”, “posterior”, “prior” → A6. “Normal”, “bell”, “shared variance” → A7. “Probability between 0 and 1”, “odds”, “classes summing to 1” → A8. “Likelihood”, “log-loss”, “cross-entropy”, “KL” → A9. “Pure”, “split”, “Gini”, “entropy” → A10. “Bootstrap”, “resample”, “out-of-bag”, “sign test” → A11. “Variance explained”, “direction”, “project” → A12.

The question says… It’s really… Toolkit
“Compute the SSE of this cluster” gaps to the centroid, squared, added A2 then A3
“Which split minimises the RSS?” each side’s mean, then squared gaps A2 then A3
“Predict with 3-NN” distances, sort, vote A1 then A2
“Posterior probability of class 1” prior × likelihood, then normalise A6 (+ A7 for Gaussians)
“Probability of default at balance 2000” linear score, then sigmoid A8
“One gradient step for logistic regression” \(\sum(p_i - y_i)x_i\), then \(\beta - \eta g\) A9 then A4
“Gini of this node” / “information gain” class proportions, impurity, weighted average A10
“Chance a point is out-of-bag” \((1 - 1/n)^n\) A11
“Density at \(x\) with \(h = 4\)” count in the window, divide by \(nh\) A2
“PVE of the first two PCs” eigenvalues over their total A12
“Stress of this embedding” distances, then squared error A1 then A3

Worked example 1: how I spotted it. “Points \((1, 1), (3, 1), (2, 4)\) form a cluster. Give its contribution to \(J_{SSE}\).”

Noun: “\(J_{SSE}\)”, a sum of squared errors (A3). Errors from what? From the cluster’s centre, which is a mean (A2). So: centroid first, then gaps, then squares.

point gap to \((2, 2)\) squared
(1, 1) (−1, −1) 2
(3, 1) (1, −1) 2
(2, 4) (0, 2) 4
total (0, 0) 8

Not \(1 + 1 + 9 + 1 + 4 + 16 = 32\), the raw-coordinate slip.

Worked example 2: how I spotted it. “A node has 6 of class A and 2 of class B. A split sends (4 A, 0 B) left and (2 A, 2 B) right. Give the decrease in Gini.”

Nouns: “Gini” (A10) and “decrease” (parent minus the weighted children, A2 for the weighting).

node proportions Gini \(= 1 - \sum p_k^2\) weight
parent 0.75, 0.25 \(1 - 0.5625 - 0.0625 = 0.375\)
left 1, 0 0 4/8
right 0.5, 0.5 0.5 4/8

Weighted children \(= 0.5\times0 + 0.5\times0.5 = 0.25\). Decrease \(= 0.375 - 0.25 = 0.125\).

2. Every common slip, on one page

The slips that cost marks

  • Squaring raw coordinates instead of gaps to the mean or centroid (K-means SSE, variance, PCA, RSS of a split).
  • Square roots: SSE, RSS and stress are sums of squares, but the stress squares distances, which do have square roots inside.
  • Old vs new centres in K-means; own cluster’s centre, not the nearest one.
  • \(n\) vs \(n - 1\) vs \(n - 2\): slides use \(\frac1n\) for PCA variance, LDA’s pooled variance uses \(\frac{1}{n - K}\).
  • Forgetting to normalise: Bayes’ rule (divide by the evidence), softmax, t-SNE similarities.
  • Sign of the gradient step: \(\beta_{\text{new}} = \beta - \eta\nabla L\).
  • \(\sigma\) vs \(\sigma^2\) in the Gaussian.
  • Unweighted children in impurity decrease: weight each child by its share of the points.
  • Parzen: divide by \(nh^p\), the box has half-width \(h/2\), edge points count.
  • PCA: centre first, unit loading vectors, PVE from variances not standard deviations.
  • Stress: compare distances, not coordinates; say \(i<j\) or ordered pairs.
  • Penalties: ridge doesn’t penalise the intercept; \(\alpha\) multiplies the number of leaves.

“Move each centre to the mean of its points” (K-means) and “predict the mean of the leaf” (regression trees) are both justified by the same fact. Which?

\(\frac{d}{dc}\sum(y_i - c)^2 = -2\sum(y_i - c) = 0 \Rightarrow c = \bar y\).

Toolkit 7.

A cluster’s SSE comes out larger than the sum of squared distances between its points. The most likely slip is:

For a cluster of \(m\) points, \(\sum_{i<j}\|x_i - x_j\|^2 = m\times\)SSE, so the SSE can’t exceed the pairwise total once \(m \ge 2\). Raw squares measure distance to the origin.
