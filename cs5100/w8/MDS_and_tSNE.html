Multidimensional scaling and t-SNE

Forget directions of variance: just place the points in 2D so that distances (MDS) or neighbourhoods (t-SNE) look like they did in the original space. Both are fitted by gradient descent. t-SNE gives by far the clearest pictures of clusters, and is for pictures only.

Question type: unsupervised: non-linear embedding for visualisation.
Week: 8 (slides 48–57).
Book: not in ISLP. Optional: Duda et al. 10.14 (MDS). This page covers everything on the slides.
Past paper: not examined so far.
Techniques on this page: the stress, MDS by gradient descent, Gaussian and \(t\) similarities, KL divergence, the digits experiment.

The problem it solves

PCA is linear: if the structure in the data is curved or clustered, two straight directions may show “a mess” (slide 55). MDS and t-SNE choose the 2D positions directly, to keep the relationships between points. MDS also works when all you have is a table of distances (no coordinates at all).

Words you’ll hear

Word Plain meaning
embedding \(\tilde x_i\) the low-dimensional position given to point \(i\)
stress sum of squared differences between original and embedded distances
similarity big when points are close, small when far (the opposite of a distance)
Gaussian similarity \(\exp(-\|x - x'\|^2/2\sigma^2)\): falls off very fast
\(t\)-similarity \(1/(1 + \|z - z'\|^2)\): falls off slowly (heavy tails)
KL divergence how different two probability tables are; 0 if equal

Technique 1: MDS and the stress (slides 49–50)

\[\text{stress} = \sum_{i,j}\big(d(x_i, x_j) - d(\tilde x_i, \tilde x_j)\big)^2 \to \min.\]

Start from random \(\tilde x_i\) and improve them by gradient descent. Summing over ordered pairs counts each pair twice; over \(i < j\) the value halves (same minimiser): say which. Variants: different distances in the two spaces, a different closeness measure, similarities instead of distances.

\(A = (0, 0)\), \(B = (3, 0)\), \(C = (0, 4)\): distances 3, 4, 5. On a line: \((0, 3, -4)\) has stress (\(i < j\)) \((5 - 7)^2 = 4\); \((0, 3, 4)\) has \((5 - 1)^2 = 16\); \((0, 2.5, -3.5)\) has \(0.25 + 0.25 + 1 = 1.5\); the best possible is \(4/3\), every pair off by \(\frac23\).

Technique 2: t-SNE (slides 51–52)

  1. Original space: Gaussian similarity \(s(x, x') = \exp\big(-\frac{\|x - x'\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\big)\), normalised, symmetrised, \(\sigma\) tuned.
  2. 2D: \(t\)-similarity \(s'(z, z') = \frac{1}{1 + \|z - z'\|^2}\), normalised.
  3. Minimise the KL divergence \(\sum p_{ij}\log\frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}\) between the two by gradient descent.

Why: the Gaussian only rewards near neighbours; the heavy-tailed \(t\) lets non-neighbours sit far apart, so clusters separate.

Distances 1 and 3 (\(\sigma = 1\)): Gaussian \(0.607\) vs \(0.011\) (factor 55); \(t\): \(0.5\) vs \(0.1\) (factor 5). KL of \(P = (0.5, 0.3, 0.2)\) from \(Q = (0.4, 0.4, 0.2)\): \(0.5\ln1.25 + 0.3\ln0.75 = 0.0253\).

Technique 3: the digits experiment (slides 53–57)

load_digits(): 1797 images, \(8 \times 8 = 64\) attributes. PCA, MDS and TSNE all have fit_transform. PCA: “a mess” (first two PCs: 28.5% of the variance). MDS: “even worse” to the lecturer’s eye. t-SNE: ten clean clusters. A 5-NN classifier on the 2D coordinates scores about 60% (PCA), 76% (MDS, 500 digits) and 98% (t-SNE).

t-SNE is for visualisation: gaps between clusters and cluster sizes mean little, it can’t project new points, and it depends on the random start and the perplexity (\(\sigma\)). PCA keeps interpretable loadings, a variance explained and a transform(). Slide 52: the \(t\)-similarity has a 1 in the numerator; some PDF viewers drop it.
