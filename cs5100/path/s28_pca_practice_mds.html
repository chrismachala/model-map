Using PCA, MDS and t-SNE

How many components to keep (PVE, scree, eigenvalue-one), why to scale first, what the USArrests loadings mean, and then two non-linear alternatives: MDS keeps distances, t-SNE keeps neighbourhoods.

Book: what to read for this stage

ISLP 12.2.3 and 12.2.4 (p510–515) for PVE, scaling, sign and how many PCs; 12.5.1 (p535–538) is your lab. MDS and t-SNE are not in ISLP: the study guide sections 7–8 and the model-map page cover slides 48–57.

1. How many PCs?

\(p\) attributes give \(p\) PCs; keep the first \(M\). PVE \(= \lambda_m/\sum\lambda_j\); choose the smallest \(M\) with cumulative PVE \(> \theta\). Scree: keep what’s left of the elbow. Eigenvalue-one: \(\lambda > 1\) (standardised data only). Interpretability. No objective rule (slide 33).

USArrests (standardised, \(\frac1n\)): \(\lambda = 2.480, 0.990, 0.357, 0.173\). PVE \(62.0, 24.7, 8.9, 4.3\%\). \(\theta = 0.8 \Rightarrow M = 2\). Eigenvalue-one: \(M = 1\) (but sklearn’s \(\frac1{n-1}\) gives \(\lambda_2 = 1.010\)).

Why does the eigenvalue-one criterion assume standardised attributes?

In raw units the threshold 1 means nothing (1 cm² vs 1 km²).

2. Scaling and loadings

Unscaled USArrests: Assault (variance ≈ 6945) takes PC1 (loading 0.995, 96.6%). Scale when units differ. Standardised loadings: PC1 \((0.54, 0.58, 0.28, 0.54)\) ≈ overall crime; PC2 \((-0.42, -0.19, 0.87, 0.17)\) ≈ urbanisation. Biplot arrows are the rows; signs are arbitrary.

The lab’s biplot is a mirror image of slide 39. Why?

Flipping a loading vector and its scores together changes nothing.

3. Reconstruction and eigenfaces

\(x_i \approx \bar x + \sum_{m \le M}z_{im}\varphi_m\); the mean squared error is the sum of the dropped \(\lambda\)’s. Eigenfaces = face loading vectors as images; a few dozen scores give a fair likeness.

PC variances \(2.48, 0.99, 0.36, 0.17\). Keeping \(M = 2\), what’s the average squared reconstruction error \(\frac1n\sum_i\|x_i - \hat x_i\|^2\)?

The dropped variances: \(0.36 + 0.17 = 0.53\).

4. MDS and t-SNE

MDS: minimise the stress \(\sum(d(x_i, x_j) - d(\tilde x_i, \tilde x_j))^2\) by gradient descent from random starts. t-SNE: Gaussian similarities \(e^{-\|x - x'\|^2/2\sigma^2}\) in the original space, \(t\)-similarities \(\frac1{1 + \|z - z'\|^2}\) in 2D, minimise the KL divergence. Heavy tails let clusters separate. Digits: PCA “a mess”, t-SNE ten clean clusters.

Two embedded points are at distance 2. What is their (unnormalised) \(t\)-similarity?

\(1/(1 + 4) = 0.2\).

Stage test

(4 marks) Explain why the variables should usually be scaled before PCA, using USArrests as an example.

PCA looks for the directions of largest variance, so an attribute with a much larger variance than the others dominates the first components simply because of its scale. In USArrests, Assault (arrests per 100,000) has variance about 6945, against about 19 for Murder and 210 for UrbanPop, so unscaled PC1 is essentially Assault (loading 0.995) and appears to explain 96.6% of the variance. Since variances depend on arbitrary units (a percentage vs a rate), each attribute is scaled to standard deviation one, which gives them equal weight; then PC1 becomes a balanced overall-crime component. If all attributes are measured in the same units, scaling may not be needed.

Which statement about t-SNE is false?

t-SNE is non-linear and gives positions only; loadings are PCA’s.
