Metrics and K-means

Week 7: no labels at all. Choose a distance, choose an objective, then improve a guess until it stops changing. K-means alternates two moves, centres to means and points to nearest centre, and always stops, though not always at the best answer.

Book: what to read for this stage

ISLP 12.1 (p503–504) and 12.4 intro + 12.4.1 K-means clustering (p520–525, Algorithm 12.2, Fig 12.8): read. 12.2 PCA and 12.3 matrix completion: skip, not on this year’s slides. Slide 78’s “Section 10.3” is the old R edition’s numbering; in ISLP it’s 12.4.

1. Metrics

\(d\) is a metric if \(d(x, y) \ge 0\); \(d(x, y) = 0\) only when \(x = y\); \(d(x, y) = d(y, x)\); and \(d(x, y) \le d(x, z) + d(z, y)\).

Minkowski \(L_k = \big(\sum_i|x_i - y_i|^k\big)^{1/k}\): Manhattan \(k = 1\), Euclidean \(k = 2\), Chebyshev \(k = \infty\) (largest difference).

\(x = (0, 3)\), \(y = (5, -9)\). Manhattan distance? (Then check: Euclidean 13, Chebyshev 12.)

\(|0 - 5| + |3 - (-9)| = 5 + 12 = 17\).

Is squared Euclidean distance a metric?

The other three axioms hold; the triangle inequality fails.

2. The SSE objective

\[J_{SSE} = \sum_{j=1}^K\sum_{x \in \omega_j}\|x - \mu_j\|^2,\qquad \mu_j = \text{mean of cluster } j.\]

3. K-means (slide 22)

  1. Randomly assign each point to one of \(K\) clusters.
  2. Repeat until nothing changes: (a) centroids = cluster means; (b) each point to the nearest centroid (ties stay).

\(1, 2, 4, 7, 8, 9\), \(K = 2\), start \(\{1, 4, 8\}\), \(\{2, 7, 9\}\). Centroids 4.33, 6 → \(\{1, 2, 4\}\), \(\{7, 8, 9\}\). Centroids 2.33, 8 → no change. \(J_{SSE} = 6.67\).

4. Why it stops; why restart; choosing K

    1. never raises \(J_{SSE}\): the mean minimises the sum of squared distances. (b) never raises it: points only move closer. Finitely many assignments, every move strictly lowers \(J\): no repeats, so it stops.
  • It stops at a local minimum depending on the start (slide 25: 320.9, 235.8 ×4, 310.9). Restart, keep the best. Not deterministic.
  • \(J_{SSE}\) always falls as \(K\) rises: choose \(K\) at the elbow.
  • A cluster can empty out: \(K\) is a maximum (data \(-3, -2, 2, 3\), start \(\{-3\}, \{3\}, \{-2, 2\}\) ends with 2 clusters).

Stage test

(4 marks) Prove that K-means always terminates.

\(J_{SSE}\) never increases: recomputing centroids replaces each centre by the mean of its cluster, which minimises the cluster’s sum of squared distances; reassigning moves a point only if another centre is strictly closer, lowering its term and leaving the others unchanged. Any change therefore strictly lowers \(J_{SSE}\), so the algorithm can never return to an earlier assignment. There are at most \(K^n\) assignments, so after finitely many iterations nothing changes and it stops (at a local, not necessarily global, minimum).
