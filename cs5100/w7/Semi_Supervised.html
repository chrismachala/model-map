Semi-supervised learning

A few labelled points, lots of unlabelled ones. Use the unlabelled data to learn what real data look like, then let that guide the learner: re-weight the labelled points, or seed K-means with them.

Question type: between supervised and unsupervised: classification helped by unlabelled data.
Week: 7 (slides 73–76).
Book: not in ISLP. This page covers the slides.
Past paper: q07 Q43–45.
Techniques on this page: the setting, re-weighting with a density estimate, seeded and constrained K-means.

The problem it solves

Labels are expensive (an expert has to look at each case); unlabelled data are cheap. With \(l\) labelled and \(u \gg l\) unlabelled observations, can we beat a classifier trained on the \(l\) alone? The unlabelled points show where the data really live.

Words you’ll hear

Word Plain meaning
labelled data \((x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_l, y_l)\)
unlabelled data \(x_{l+1}, \dots, x_{l+u}\), no \(y\)
seeds the labelled points, used to start K-means
seeded K-means seeds only set the starting centres
constrained K-means seeds also keep their cluster throughout

Technique 1: re-weighting (slide 75)

Learners minimise the error on the labelled points with equal weights, but we care about future data. If the few labelled points over-represent some regions, equal weights mislead. Use the unlabelled data to estimate the density (density estimation) and weight each labelled point by how common its region really is; minimise the weighted error.

Technique 2: seeded and constrained K-means (slide 76)

Both start cluster \(j\)’s centre at the mean of the seeds labelled \(j\) (no random start, and the cluster numbers match the classes).

  • Seeded: then run ordinary K-means on everything; seeds may move.
  • Constrained: seeds stay in their labelled cluster at every step; only unlabelled points move.

Seeded. Seeds A at \(1, 3\); B at \(9\). Unlabelled \(2, 6, 7, 10\). Centres A \(= 2\), B \(= 9\). Assign: \(1, 2, 3 \to\) A; \(6\) (4 vs 3), \(7, 9, 10 \to\) B. Centres 2 and 8. 6 is 4 vs 2: still B. Done: A \(= \{1, 2, 3\}\), B \(= \{6, 7, 9, 10\}\).

The two differ only when ordinary reassignment would move a seed to another cluster (a mislabelled or unusual seed). Trust the labels: constrained. Labels may be noisy: seeded.
