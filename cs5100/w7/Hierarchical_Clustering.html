Hierarchical clustering

Start with every point on its own. Repeatedly merge the two closest clusters until one is left, and draw each merge at the height where it happened. The result is a tree; cut it wherever you like to get any number of clusters.

Question type: unsupervised: clustering (a tree of nested partitions, the dendrogram).
Week: 7 (slides 29–49).
Book: ISLP 12.4.2 (p525–532), Algorithm 12.3, Table 12.3.
Past paper: 2023 Q5(a)iii (single linkage, furthest pair) and 2023 Q5(b) (complete and centroid linkage by hand); q07 Q19–30.
Techniques on this page: reading a dendrogram, agglomerative clustering by hand, the four linkages, chaining, inversions, cutting, divisive clustering, K-means vs hierarchical.

The problem it solves

K-means makes you pick \(K\) first and gives a different answer from each random start. Hierarchical clustering builds every clustering at once, from \(n\) singletons up to one big group, deterministically. You choose the number of clusters afterwards by looking at the tree.

Words you’ll hear

Word Plain meaning
dendrogram the merge tree; the height of each join = the dissimilarity at which it happened
agglomerative (AHC) bottom-up: start with singletons, merge
divisive top-down: start with one cluster, split
linkage how to measure the distance between two clusters
chaining single linkage stringing points into a long cluster
inversion a merge drawn lower than an earlier one (centroid linkage)

Technique 1: the algorithm (slide 32, Algorithm 12.3)

  1. Each observation is its own cluster; compute all \(n(n-1)/2\) pairwise distances.
  2. For \(i = n, \dots, 2\): fuse the two clusters with the smallest linkage, at that height; update the linkages.

Read a dendrogram vertically only: two leaves drawn side by side aren’t necessarily close (slide 38, “2 is not closer to 9”).

Technique 2: the four linkages (slides 33–34, Table 12.3)

Linkage Distance between clusters Behaviour
single closest pair chaining, unbalanced trees
complete furthest pair compact clusters, balanced
average mean of all \(n_jn_k\) pairs in between
centroid distance between the means can invert

2023 Q5(b). \(\omega_1 = \{(3, 4), (5, 2)\}\), \(\omega_2 = \{(-1, -1), (2, -4), (2, -1)\}\). Make the table of all six cross distances first: \(\sqrt{41}, \sqrt{65}, \sqrt{26}\) from \((3, 4)\); \(\sqrt{45}, \sqrt{45}, \sqrt{18}\) from \((5, 2)\). Complete \(= \sqrt{65} = \mathbf{8.06}\). Single \(= \sqrt{18} = 4.24\). Average \(= 6.20\). Centroid: \((4, 3)\) to \((1, -2)\) \(= \sqrt{34} = \mathbf{5.83}\).

Slide 44, single linkage in 1D. \(\{1, 3, 4, 13, 28, 29\}\), ties broken by the larger mean. Merges: \(\{28, 29\}\) at 1, \(\{3, 4\}\) at 1, \(+1\) at 2, \(+13\) at 9, all at 15. In 1D with single linkage only neighbouring gaps matter.

Technique 3: the two weaknesses

Chaining (2023 Q5(a)iii: true). Single linkage merges two clusters as soon as any two of their points are close. A long line of points 1 apart, plus a few small blobs further away, merges into one long cluster first, so with 5 clusters the two furthest points can share a cluster. (Lab exercise 3 builds this.)

Inversions (centroid). \(A = (0, 0)\), \(B = (2, 0)\), \(C = (1, 1.8)\): merge \(A, B\) at 2; their centroid \((1, 0)\) is only 1.8 from \(C\), so the next merge is lower. Single, complete and average never do this.

Technique 4: cutting, divisive, and K-means vs hierarchical

Cutting (slide 35): a horizontal line at height \(h\); the branches it crosses are the clusters. For \(k\) clusters, cut between the \((n - k)\)-th and \((n - k + 1)\)-th merge heights.

Divisive (slides 46–48): start with everything, repeatedly split the “worst” cluster (e.g. with 2-means). There are \(2^{m-1} - 1\) ways to split \(m\) points, so it needs heuristics; agglomerative is more popular.

K-means hierarchical
output one partition a tree of partitions
\(K\) chosen before chosen after (cut)
randomness random start; restart deterministic (bar ties)
cost fast all pairwise distances, \(O(n^2)\) memory

The lab’s AgglomerativeClustering defaults to Ward linkage (not on the slides) and has no centroid option. Always pass linkage='single', 'complete' or 'average'.
