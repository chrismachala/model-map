PCA, MDS and t-SNE

Week 8's three ways to draw high-dimensional data in 2D, assembled from pieces you know: PCA is "maximise the variance of a dot product" (A12), MDS is "squared error between distances, by gradient descent" (A3, A4), t-SNE is "Gaussian bumps and a cross-entropy-like divergence, by gradient descent" (A7, A9, A4).

Built from

A12 covariance and projection \(\varphi^T\Sigma\varphi\) · A1 dot products and distances · A2 the mean (centring) · A3 squared error (stress, reconstruction) · A4 gradient descent · A7 the Gaussian (t-SNE’s similarity) · A9 KL divergence, a relative of cross-entropy. Full pages: dimensionality reduction, PCA, PCA in practice, MDS and t-SNE; week-order stages: 27, 28.

1. PCA

Centre with the mean (A2), form \(\Sigma = \frac1nX^TX\) (A12), maximise \(\varphi^T\Sigma\varphi\) over unit \(\varphi\): the eigenvectors in order of eigenvalue. Scores are dot products \(z_{im} = \varphi_m^Tx_i\) (A1). PVE \(= \lambda_m/\sum\lambda_j\). Reconstruction \(\hat x_i = \bar x + \sum_{m \le M}z_{im}\varphi_m\) has mean squared error (A3) equal to the dropped eigenvalues: PCA is also “the \(M\)-dimensional plane with the smallest squared error”.

2. MDS

Stress \(= \sum_{i<j}\big(d_{ij} - \|\tilde x_i - \tilde x_j\|\big)^2\): squared error (A3) between distances (A1), minimised by gradient descent (A4) from random starts, so local minima, as in K-means and neural networks.

3. t-SNE

Original space: Gaussian-shaped similarities \(e^{-\|x - x'\|^2/2\sigma^2}\) (A7), normalised into probabilities \(p_{ij}\). 2D: \(t\)-similarities \(\frac1{1 + \|z - z'\|^2}\), normalised into \(q_{ij}\). Loss: \(\text{KL}(P\|Q) = \sum p_{ij}\log\frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}\), which is the cross-entropy \(-\sum p_{ij}\log q_{ij}\) (A9) minus a constant. Minimised by gradient descent (A4).

Which toolkit piece does PCA not use?

PCA has no labels and no probabilities: centring (mean), covariance, and projections (dot products).
