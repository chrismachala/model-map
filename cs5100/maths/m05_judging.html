Judging and comparing models

Three evaluation topics from weeks 3 and 5, and none of them needs new maths. Confusion matrices and ROC curves are counting; the bias-variance decomposition is squared error; the sign test is the binomial from Toolkit 6.

Built from

A2 proportions of counts (TPR, FPR, accuracy, error rate) · A6 conditional probability (TPR \(= P(\hat y = 1\mid y = 1)\)) and the binomial · A3 the bias-variance decomposition · A5 / A11 flexibility and validation. Full pages: judging classifiers, bias and variance, comparing algorithms; week-order stages: 12, 19, 20.

1. Confusion matrix and ROC: counting

Predict 1 when \(\hat p(x) \ge \xi\) (A8’s probability, any threshold \(\xi\)). Count TP, FN, FP, TN. Then

\[\text{TPR} = \frac{TP}{P},\quad \text{FPR} = \frac{FP}{N},\quad \text{TNR} = 1 - \text{FPR},\quad \text{accuracy} = \frac{TP + TN}{P + N}.\]

Each is a proportion (A2) and a conditional probability (A6). Sliding \(\xi\) from 1 to 0 traces the ROC curve (FPR, TPR); the AUC is the probability that a random positive gets a higher score than a random negative.

2. Bias-variance: squared error

Test MSE at \(x_0\) = bias\(^2\) + variance + noise (A3). More flexibility: less bias, more variance; the test error is U-shaped and every knob in A5 moves you along it.

At \(x_0\): bias\(^2 = 0.25\), variance \(= 0.75\), noise \(\sigma^2 = 0.5\). What is the expected test MSE?

\(0.25 + 0.75 + 0.5 = 1.5\).

3. Comparing two algorithms: the binomial

On the test points where A and B disagree (\(N\) of them), count A’s wins \(k\). If they’re equally good, \(k\sim\text{Binomial}(N, \frac12)\) (A6), and the p-value is \(P(Y \ge k) = \sum_{j\ge k}\binom Nj2^{-N}\). Reject “equally good” if it’s below the significance level (e.g. 5%).
