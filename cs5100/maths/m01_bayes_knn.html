Bayes classifier and KNN

Week 1's two classifiers, assembled from parts you already have: Bayes' rule and argmax give the ideal, and distances plus a vote give KNN, the practical copy. Swap the vote for an average and you have week 2's KNN regression.

Built from

A6 Bayes’ rule and probability (the target \(P(Y = k\mid x)\) and the argmax rule) · A1 distances (who the neighbours are) · A2 averages and votes (the estimate) · A3 squared error (scoring KNN regression) · A5 / A11 the knob \(K\), chosen on validation data. Full pages: Bayes classifier, KNN, KNN regression; week-order stage: Bayes classifier and KNN.

1. The assembly

\[\underbrace{\hat Y(x_0) = \arg\max_k}_{\text{A2 vote}}\ \underbrace{\tilde P(Y = k\mid x_0)}_{\text{A6 target}} = \underbrace{\frac1K\sum_{i\in N_K(x_0)}\mathbb 1(y_i = k)}_{\text{A2 proportion}},\qquad N_K(x_0) = \underbrace{\text{the } K \text{ closest by } \|x_i - x_0\|_2}_{\text{A1 distance}}\]

  • The Bayes classifier is the same rule with the true \(P(Y = k\mid x_0)\): unbeatable, but never available. Its error \(1 - \max_kP(Y = k\mid x_0)\) is the floor.
  • KNN regression replaces the vote by the average: \(\hat y(x_0) = \frac1K\sum_{i\in N_K}y_i\), scored by test MSE.
  • Nothing is fitted. KNN stores the data; all the work happens at prediction time.

2. The knob

\(K = 1\) is the most flexible (training error 0, jagged boundary, high variance); \(K = n\) predicts the overall majority everywhere (high bias). Choose \(K\) on a validation set (A11). Inputs must be on comparable scales (A1), which is why the week 2 lab normalises before KNN regression.

What happens to KNN’s training error rate as \(K\) goes from \(n\) down to 1?

Training error is a bad guide to \(K\) for exactly this reason; validation error is U-shaped.

Stage test

KNN regression with \(K = 3\). The three nearest neighbours of \(x_0\) have responses \(4, 5, 6\). Predict \(\hat y(x_0)\).

The average, \((4 + 5 + 6)/3 = 5\) (A2).

(4 marks; 2023 Q2.) Explain how KNN classifies a new point, and how it can be changed to do regression. How does \(K\) affect flexibility?

KNN computes the distance (usually Euclidean, \(\sqrt{\sum_j(x_j - x_{0j})^2}\)) from \(x_0\) to every training point and takes the \(K\) closest. For classification it estimates \(P(Y = k\mid x_0)\) by the fraction of those neighbours with label \(k\) and predicts the most common class, imitating the Bayes classifier. For regression it predicts the mean of the neighbours’ \(y\) values. With \(K = 1\) the fit follows every training point (training error 0, high variance); larger \(K\) averages over more points, giving a smoother, less flexible fit with more bias and less variance. \(K\) is chosen by validation error.
