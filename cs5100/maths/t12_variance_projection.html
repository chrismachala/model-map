Variance, covariance and projections

The variance measures spread along one attribute; the covariance matrix measures it along every direction at once. Project the data onto a unit vector with a dot product and the spread along that direction is φᵀΣφ. That single formula is all of PCA.

Book and slides

ISLP 12.2.1 (eq. 12.1–12.3: normalised linear combinations and their sample variance). Week 8 slides 21–27. Variance with \(\frac1n\) throughout, like the slides; scikit-learn and pandas use \(\frac1{n-1}\), which changes the numbers by \(\frac{n}{n-1}\) but not the directions. The general definition of eigenvectors is optional (slide 26).

1. Variance and covariance

For values \(z_1, \dots, z_n\) with mean \(\mu\): \(\text{Var} = \frac1n\sum_i(z_i - \mu)^2\) (the squared error of predicting every value by the mean, A3). For two attributes, the covariance \(\frac1n\sum_i(x_{ij} - \mu_j)(x_{ik} - \mu_k)\) is positive when they rise together.

Collect them all: for centred data (each column’s mean subtracted),

\[\boxed{\Sigma = \frac1nX^TX}\qquad\text{(variances on the diagonal, covariances off it)}\]

and the scatter matrix is \(S = \sum_i(x_i - \mu)(x_i - \mu)^T = n\Sigma\). Standardising (dividing each column by its standard deviation) turns \(\Sigma\) into the correlation matrix.

\((3, 1), (1, 3), (-1, -3), (-3, -1)\): means 0, \(\Sigma_{11} = \Sigma_{22} = 20/4 = 5\), \(\Sigma_{12} = 12/4 = 3\).

Centred data \((2, 1), (-2, -1), (1, 3), (-1, -3)\). Compute the covariance \(\Sigma_{12}\) (with \(\frac1n\)).

\(\frac14(2 + 2 + 3 + 3) = 2.5\).

2. Projection onto a direction

A unit vector \(\varphi\) (\(\|\varphi\| = 1\)) is a direction. The dot product (A1) \(z_i = \varphi^Tx_i\) is the signed length of \(x_i\)’s shadow on that direction: its projection. Two directions are orthogonal iff \(\varphi^T\psi = 0\).

For centred data the projections have mean 0 and variance

\[\boxed{\frac1n\sum_i(\varphi^Tx_i)^2 = \varphi^T\Sigma\varphi.}\]

Same \(\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}5 & 3\\3 & 5\end{pmatrix}\). Along \((1, 0)\): \(5\). Along \(\frac1{\sqrt2}(1, 1)\): \(\frac12(5 + 3 + 3 + 5) = 8\). Along \(\frac1{\sqrt2}(1, -1)\): \(2\). Along \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\): \(5 + 3\sin2\theta\).

\(\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\1 & 3\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\varphi = (0.6, 0.8)\). Compute \(\varphi^T\Sigma\varphi\).

\(\Sigma\varphi = (2(0.6) + 0.8,\ 0.6 + 3(0.8)) = (2, 3)\), then \(\varphi \cdot (2, 3) = 1.2 + 2.4 = 3.6\). (Or term by term: \(2(0.36) + 2(1)(0.48) + 3(0.64) = 3.6\).)

3. The directions of largest spread: eigenvectors

Maximising \(\varphi^T\Sigma\varphi\) over unit vectors gives the eigenvector of \(\Sigma\) with the largest eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\), and the maximum is \(\lambda_1\). The next best direction orthogonal to it is the next eigenvector. The eigenvalues add up to the total variance (the diagonal sum). For \(2 \times 2\): \(\lambda = \frac{a + c}{2} \pm \sqrt{(\frac{a - c}{2})^2 + b^2}\); with equal diagonal, \(\lambda = a \pm b\) along the diagonals.

Where How

PVE (w8) \(\lambda_m/\sum\lambda_j\), using “eigenvalues add up to the total variance”
LDA (w3) the shared (pooled) covariance of the classes; Fisher’s LDA is a projection too (w8 slide 15)

Bias-variance (w5), bagging (w6) variance of a prediction; \(\rho\sigma^2\) is a covariance between trees
K-means (w7), reconstruction (w8) the mean minimises squared error; PCA’s reconstruction error = dropped eigenvalues
